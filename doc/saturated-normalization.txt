Normalization and BFU saturation bypass

Qualified numerical domain

NORM and NORMF now accept arithmetic saturation (SM) in ordinary 56-bit operation
with the six established scaling/RM combinations. The paths differ: NORM uses a
single-bit arithmetic step that can saturate; NORMF uses the Bit Field Unit (BFU)
and bypasses SM. Multi-bit ASL/ASR are BFU operations too, even when the encoded
count is one. Their previously incorrect limiting behavior is corrected in this
increment. Native admission and public layouts/contracts stay unchanged.

Primary source: DSP56300 Family Manual Rev.5 sections 3.1.5 and 3.2.3/Table 3-1,
plus instruction pages 13-14..17 and 13-146..148. Section 3.1.5 identifies the BFU
operations; section 3.2.3 excludes all BFU results from arithmetic saturation.
Reading the instruction pages alone missed this distinction in the previous
qualification. Reference copies/extracts and the pre-review design remain private.
Manufacturer source: https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/reference-manual/DSP56300FM.pdf

The private ShiftControl carries an explicit saturation choice. Defaults keep
single-bit/NORM behavior; immediate/register ASL/ASR and NORMF disable it. Path
selection depends on instruction form, not runtime count. No transient clearing
of architectural SM, public state field or mode-key relaxation is introduced.
Other numerical domains still diagnose; DIV SM remains unsupported. Other BFU
classes retain their established qualified bypass or explicit admission gaps.
This change does not silently admit untested logical operations.

Counts outside NORMF's specified -55..+56 and ASL/ASR's 0..55 domain retain
explicit software diagnostics, not claimed hardware traps. SB/SC/DM, reserved
scaling, complete combinations, physical flags and sequence/IRQ/pipeline/timing
remain open. In particular the existing NORMF V bit-index inconsistency and
NORM NOP-status hardware confirmation are not resolved by these tests.

Independent binary expectations

The new standalone generator links only the fixture codec. Its test oracle uses
raw56 one-bit shifts, an explicit E/U/Z action table and an independent table-
indexed limiter only for saturating forms. The core uses aligned64 shifts/sign
tests. Expected state never comes from executing the emulator. Carry is restored
independently for normalization; BFU shifts retain their shifted-out Carry.
Exponent steps use a separate modular coordinate, not the production AGU helper.

79552 original cases occupy two archives under the unchanged 65536-case bound:
normalization-sm-fast.d56 (50240), normalization-sm-iteration.d56 (29312).
The generator accepts fast/iteration and reports selected and complete counts.
Both archives reproduce exactly; no matrix cases are removed to fit the bound.

Matrices cover every admitted NORMF count/source/destination and six scaling/RM
choices over four declared edge patterns, separate selected-count/source
all-CCR matrices and NORM's all-CCR/all-eight-R/both-destination matrix. All
extension bytes/MSP signs test -1/0/+1 and NORM decisions; individual/complemented
raw bits include full-width right shifts. Exponent boundary coordinates, ignored
modifiers, REP current-source/flag reevaluation with every partial prefix, DO
final/continuing iterations and saved LF/FV/stack wrap are explicit. These are
finite matrices, not all operand/state tuples.

Old iteration ordinal31148 and normalization ordinal28451 become successes with
identical initial programs/registers/input. The latter now tests SM bypass.
The old saturated-shifts and bit-operations producers correct 12870 and two BFU
register expectations respectively, with 24 revised REP labels, without changing
original inputs. Complete record auditing permits only A/B/SR result changes for
those declared BFU forms and verifies every unrelated serialized byte. The other
48 old archives remain byte-identical. Historical green tests were insufficient because their mode
interpretation was wrong; pre-review artifacts are retained as diagnostics and
are not final qualification.

Public-Core boundaries

Host checks cover exact native/general prefixes, complete registers and real
native/helper/dispatch/base-cycle counts. Same-PC ordinary/SM/ordinary/SM runs
cannot retain stale assumptions. Equal one-bit shifts through parallel ALU,
immediate BFU, register BFU and NORMF distinguish saturation/bypass independently.
NORMF count zero clears stale result flags without limiting the raw accumulator.
Out-of-domain counts, other modes and DIV SM retain fail-before-retirement
expectations. Callbacks observe canonical earlier native work, accept/reject a
parallel load, patch the following P instruction and verify exact partial effects
and fail-stop state without replay. Reference/cached/JIT, optimizer on/off and
block limits 1/64 use independently modeled expectations. Full IRQ admission,
every alias/transfer and physical timing remain unqualified. All 118 ISA rows
remain partial; the full objective is unchanged and active.


Continuous regression scope

The four continuous programs do not measure sustained SM-normalization capacity.
Their final-source comparison keeps every guest/native/helper/move/batch/AVX2
count and executable page size equal. All 16 full states agree, but seven matched
packages fail, three in candidate runs. The final default gate fails spectral at
1.222611 s / 209.96 actual MIPS, with process CPU 0.758929 s. All four default block
budgets have misses: 71/39/52/31. Large wall/CPU differences suggest off-CPU delay;
its cause is not isolated and no failed realtime budget is waived. No repeat
capture is used to obtain a passing sample. See continuous-bench.txt for every
scope, CPU difference, PMU miss regression and retained historical failure.

PMU instruction/load counts are essentially unchanged and matched CPU changes
are small; no causal speed or energy benefit is claimed. Full process counters
include setup/cold/warm/state checks and do not establish isolated JIT locality
or explain wall-time waits. Numerical accuracy improves while the performance,
complete-ISA and other full-objective requirements remain active and incomplete.
